Every gem has a story.

Where it forms, what gives it color, and how well it stands up to everyday wear. Get to know them here, then come see them in person.

About hardness: gems are rated from 1 to 10 on the Mohs scale, and diamond is a 10. Stones around 7 and up handle an everyday ring well. Softer gems like opal, pearl and turquoise are lovely in earrings and pendants, or in rings worn with a little care.

ACDEFGIJKLMOPRSTZ

Alexandrite

June birthstoneHardness 8.5

The color-change variety of chrysoberyl: bluish green in daylight, purplish red under lamplight. Hard, durable, and rare in fine quality.

Amber

Hardness 2 to 2.5

Fossilized tree resin in warm golden and orange-brown tones. Some pieces hold tiny insects or plants trapped millions of years ago. Soft, so best in pendants and earrings.

Amethyst

February birthstoneHardness 7

The purple variety of quartz. Its name comes from the Greek for “not drunk,” from an old belief that it kept the wearer sober.

Ametrine

Hardness 7

A rare quartz that holds two colors in one stone, amethyst purple and citrine gold, often cut to show both side by side.

Aquamarine

March birthstoneHardness 7.5 to 8

The sea-blue to greenish-blue variety of beryl. Crystals can grow large enough to cut gems of more than 100 carats.

Citrine

November birthstoneHardness 7

Yellow to orange quartz, colored by traces of iron. One of the most popular yellow gems, and an affordable alternative to yellow sapphire and topaz.

Diamond

April birthstoneHardness 10

The hardest natural material, made of a single element: carbon. Most diamonds formed deep in the earth over a billion years ago. See our diamond guide.

Emerald

May birthstoneHardness 7.5 to 8

The most prized variety of beryl, treasured by Inca kings, Mughal emperors and pharaohs. Most emeralds have visible inclusions, which jewelers call the jardin, or garden.

Fancy color diamond

Hardness 10

Only about one diamond in 10,000 shows natural color strong enough to be called a fancy color. Yellow is the most common; pink, blue and green are among the rarest.

Garnet

January birthstoneHardness 6.5 to 7.5

Not one gem but a family of related minerals, in nearly every color: deep red pyrope, orange spessartine, and vivid green tsavorite and demantoid.

Iolite

Hardness 7 to 7.5

Called cordierite by geologists. Iolite shows different colors from different angles, violet blue from one side and almost clear from another.

Jade

Hardness 6 to 7

Two minerals, jadeite and nephrite, share the name. Prized from ancient China to the Maya and Aztecs, jade is tough rather than hard, which is why it carves so beautifully.

Kunzite

Hardness 6.5 to 7

The pink to violet variety of spodumene, colored by traces of manganese and named for gemologist George F. Kunz. Its color can fade in strong sun, so it's an evening stone.

Lapis lazuli

Hardness 5 to 5.5

Deep blue stone flecked with golden pyrite that glitters like stars. It's been carved into jewelry, and ground into ultramarine paint, for thousands of years.

Moonstone

June birthstoneHardness 6 to 6.5

A feldspar famous for its soft, floating blue glow, called adularescence. A favorite of Art Nouveau jewelers.

Morganite

Hardness 7.5 to 8

Peach to pink beryl, a cousin of emerald and aquamarine. Its blush comes from traces of manganese, and it's become a favorite for engagement rings.

Opal

October birthstoneHardness 5.5 to 6.5

Tiny stacked spheres of silica split light into flashes of color, called play-of-color. Softer than most gems, so set it with care or wear it in pendants and earrings.

Pearl

June birthstoneHardness 2.5 to 4.5

The only gem grown by a living creature, an oyster or mussel, in salt water or fresh. Most pearls today are cultured. Store them apart from harder jewelry.

Peridot

August birthstoneHardness 6.5 to 7

The yellow-green gem form of olivine. It forms deep in the earth and rises in volcanic rock, and a few crystals have even been found in meteorites.

Rose quartz

Hardness 7

Soft pink quartz, usually cloudy and translucent from microscopic inclusions. Clear, well-formed pink crystals are rare.

Sapphire

September birthstoneHardness 9

Corundum in every color but red, which is ruby. Blue is the classic, but sapphires also come in pink, yellow, green, purple and orange. Second only to diamond in hardness.

Spinel

August birthstoneHardness 8

Long mistaken for ruby: some famous “rubies” in royal crowns are really spinel. Hard and bright, in reds, pinks, blues and purples.

Sunstone

Hardness 6 to 6.5

A feldspar with a glittering, coppery shimmer called aventurescence, from tiny reflective platelets inside the stone.

Tanzanite

December birthstoneHardness 6 to 7

The blue-violet variety of zoisite, discovered in 1967 and named for Tanzania, still the only place it's mined.

Topaz

November birthstoneHardness 8

Comes in colorless, pale blue, yellow, orange, pink and prized golden “imperial” shades. Most bright blue topaz starts out colorless and is treated.

Tourmaline

October birthstoneHardness 7 to 7.5

Comes in more colors than any other gem, sometimes several in one crystal, like pink-and-green watermelon tourmaline. The name comes from the Sinhalese turmali, meaning mixed.

Turquoise

December birthstoneHardness 5 to 6

Sky-blue to green stone, often veined with its host rock, prized for thousands of years from Egypt to the American Southwest.

Zircon

December birthstoneHardness 6.5 to 7.5

Not cubic zirconia: zircon is a natural gem and one of the oldest minerals on earth. Colorless zircon has so much fire it was long mistaken for diamond.
